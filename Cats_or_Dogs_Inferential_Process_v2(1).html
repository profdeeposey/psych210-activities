Cats or Dogs?

An activity about how researchers use samples to learn about populations.

Imagine a population of 10,000 people. Every person prefers either cats or dogs.

Research Question:
What percentage of people in this population prefer dogs?

There is one problem: You cannot study all 10,000 people.

How could you answer the research question?

STOP — Discuss with your group and wait for your instructor.

Take a Random Sample

Instead of studying all 10,000 people, randomly select 10 people from the population.

How many prefer dogs? out of 10
What percentage of your sample prefers dogs? %
Based on your sample, what percentage of the population would you estimate prefers dogs?
Our population estimate: %
STOP — Wait for class discussion.

Try It Again

Your first sample gave you one estimate. What happens if you repeatedly select 10 people from the same population?

STOP — All five samples came from the same population. Why aren't the estimates identical?

Increase the Sample Size

One way researchers can reduce sampling variability is to use a larger sample. This time, take two random samples of 50 people.

Compare: How do the two n = 50 estimates compare with your five n = 10 estimates? Does a larger sample guarantee a correct estimate?
STOP — Discuss with your group and wait for your instructor.

Go Big

Now take two random samples of 500 people.

Before you sample: Compared with your smaller samples, what do you predict will happen to these estimates?

Do you know the actual population percentage yet?

Make Your Final Inference

You have never observed the population. You have observed five random samples of 10, two samples of 50, and two samples of 500.

Looking at all of your sample results, what percentage of the population do you estimate prefers dogs?
Our final estimate: %

Your final inference: % of the population prefers dogs.

STOP — Wait for class discussion. Do not reveal the population until instructed.

Reveal the Population

Population = 10,000 people

🐶 5,700 prefer dogs — 57%

🐱 4,300 prefer cats — 43%

Your final estimate was %. The actual population value is 57%.

The Inferential Process

1. Ask a research question about a population.
What percentage of the population prefers dogs?
↓
2. Draw a random sample from the population.
We could not study all 10,000 people, so we studied smaller groups selected at random.
↓
3. Use the sample to answer the research question.
We calculated the percentage preferring dogs in each sample.
↓
4. Generalize from the sample back to the population.
We used what was true of our sample to estimate what was true of the population.

The complication: Sampling Error

Different random samples from the same population produced different estimates.

Increasing sample size reduced the variability of the estimates, but did not eliminate it.
